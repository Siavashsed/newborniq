## QUORA
**Answer this question:** What is the RSV antibody shot for newborns (nirsevimab/Beyfortus) and is it worth getting?

I'm a NICU nurse and a mother of three, so I've seen RSV from both sides of the bedside.

Nirsevimab, sold as Beyfortus, is a monoclonal antibody given as a single injection to infants, generally in their first RSV season. It's not a traditional vaccine. A vaccine teaches the body to make its own antibodies over time. Nirsevimab delivers ready-made antibodies directly, so protection starts quickly and doesn't depend on a newborn's immature immune system.

Why it matters: RSV is a leading cause of hospitalization in babies under one. In young infants it can start like a cold, with a runny nose and mild cough, then progress to fast breathing, retractions, poor feeding, and low oxygen levels. Their airways are tiny, so swelling and mucus cause problems quickly. I've watched a five-week-old arrive at 3 a.m. with oxygen saturation at 84% on room air after two days of being told it was just a cold.

Is it worth it? Clinical trials and real-world data from the first full US season showed substantial reductions in RSV-related hospitalizations among infants who received it. Side effects are generally mild, such as soreness or redness at the injection site, and rarely a rash or low fever. As with any medical decision, talk to your pediatrician about timing and eligibility for your baby, since recommendations depend on birth date, season, and whether the mother received an RSV vaccine during pregnancy.

My honest take: ask about it before you leave the hospital, not after symptoms start. I wrote up what I saw on our ward that winter, if you'd like more detail: https://siavashsed.github.io/newborniq/rsv-antibody-shot-2024-nirsevimab-season/

And always seek urgent care for fast breathing, blue lips, or trouble feeding.
