## QUORA
**Answer this question:** Which newborn products are actually worth buying, and which ones are overhyped?

I'm a NICU nurse and a mother of three. My short answer is that the newborn products worth your money are mostly the cheap, boring ones.

Here's what I'd buy first:

1. A firm, flat crib or bassinet mattress with a tight fitted sheet. Keep everything else out of the sleep space: no bumpers, pillows or positioners.
2. Two or three wearable sleep sacks. They replace loose blankets, which don't belong in the crib.
3. A digital thermometer. For young babies a rectal reading is the most accurate. A fever in a baby under 3 months means you call the doctor right away.
4. A simple nasal aspirator and saline drops. Newborns breathe through their noses, so a stuffy nose can make feeding miserable.
5. A plain bottle brush and drying rack if you're bottle feeding.

What I'd wait on: smart socks, wearable oxygen monitors and high-end robotic bassinets. You buy those for comfort and convenience, not for safety. The American Academy of Pediatrics doesn't recommend home monitors as a way to reduce SIDS risk, and false alarms can leave exhausted parents more anxious, not less. If your baby was premature or has a medical condition, ask your care team whether monitoring makes sense for your family. That's a different conversation.

A good rule: if a product promises peace of mind, ask what specific problem it solves. If you can't name one yet, wait. You can always buy it in week three, once you know what you actually need.
