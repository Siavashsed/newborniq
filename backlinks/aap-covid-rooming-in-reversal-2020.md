## QUORA
**Answer this question:** Can a mother with COVID-19 stay in the same room as her newborn baby?

Yes, in most cases. On July 22, 2020, the American Academy of Pediatrics updated its guidance to say that a mother with COVID-19 can keep her newborn in the room with her if she takes a few precautions.

The core precautions are:

1. Wear a mask whenever you are holding, feeding or sitting close to the baby.
2. Wash your hands thoroughly before touching the baby.
3. When practical, keep the crib or bassinet about six feet from your bed while the baby sleeps.

The same guidance encouraged breastfeeding with a mask and clean hands. Another option was to pump with clean equipment so a healthy caregiver could feed the baby.

Why the change? Interim guidance in spring 2020 had leaned toward temporarily separating COVID-positive mothers from their newborns. As data came in, newborn infection rates looked low when mothers used basic precautions. Separation also had real costs: lost skin-to-skin contact, harder starts to breastfeeding and a lot of distress for parents.

A few practical notes if this applies to you:

- Ask your hospital about its current policy before delivery. Hospitals applied the guidance differently, and policies have kept changing since 2020.
- If you feel too sick to care for the baby safely, a healthy support person can do most of the hands-on care while you stay close.
- Babies born early or with medical problems may need a different plan, so follow your baby's care team.
