## QUORA
**Answer this question:** Should I feed my newborn on a schedule or on demand?

For a healthy, full-term baby, on demand is the better default, with one guardrail: in the first couple of weeks, don't go more than about 3 hours between feeds, even if you have to wake the baby.

Why not a strict schedule? Newborns don't eat in neat, equal portions. They cluster feed in the evenings and go through growth spurts, often around 2 to 3 weeks and again around 6 weeks. Some feeds are full meals and others are snacks. For breastfed babies, frequent feeding on cue also helps build milk supply in those early weeks. With a rigid chart, you end up either holding off a hungry baby or waking a content one for no reason.

Watch the baby, not the clock. Early hunger cues include rooting, sucking on hands, lip smacking, and turning toward the breast or bottle. Crying is a late cue, and a frantic baby often latches poorly.

How do you know it's working? Look for:
- About 8 to 12 feeds in 24 hours
- 6 or more wet diapers a day by around day 5
- Regular stools
- Back to birth weight by roughly 10 to 14 days

There's a good piece that walks through the reasoning, including why the laminated-chart approach tends to backfire: https://siavashsed.github.io/newborniq/newborn-feeding-schedule-vs-on-demand/

When a schedule is the right call: preemies and babies with jaundice, low blood sugar, or slow weight gain often need timed feeds set by their pediatrician. In those cases, follow the medical plan, not general advice.

Once your baby is gaining well and your pediatrician gives the okay, you can usually stop waking for feeds and let longer stretches happen at night. Until then, feed on cues first, with the 3-hour floor as your backstop.
