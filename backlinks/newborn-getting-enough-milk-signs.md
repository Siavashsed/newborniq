## QUORA
**Answer this question:** How can I tell if my newborn is getting enough breast milk?

I'm a NICU nurse and a mother of three, and I can tell you that knowing the signs didn't stop me from panicking at 3 a.m. with my second baby. She nursed for 40 minutes and was still rooting, and I was sure she was starving. So here is what I now trust over any timer.

1. Diapers. In the first days, expect the count to climb: roughly one wet diaper on day one, two on day two, three on day three, and so on. By about day five, look for five or more truly wet diapers a day. Stools should change from dark meconium to green, then to yellow and seedy. This is the single most practical daily check.

2. Weight. Most newborns lose a few percent of their birth weight in the first days, then start gaining. Many are back to birth weight by about two weeks. Your pediatrician will track this, and it's the most objective measure you have.

3. Baby's behavior after a feed. A baby who is getting enough is usually relaxed afterward, with soft, open hands and a loose body, and is alert and content for some stretch of time. You'll also often hear regular swallowing during the feed.

What doesn't tell you much: how long a feed lasted, how often baby wants to eat, or constant rooting. Cluster feeding is normal, and babies also suck for comfort.

Call your pediatrician promptly if you see very few wet diapers, dark urine or brick-dust-colored crystals after the first few days, yellowing skin that's getting worse, or a baby who is too sleepy to wake for feeds.
