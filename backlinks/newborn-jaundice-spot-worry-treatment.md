## QUORA
**Answer this question:** How can I tell if my newborn's jaundice is normal or something I should worry about?

I'm a NICU nurse and a mother of three, and I still got this wrong once, so let me give you the practical version.

First, the reassuring part: jaundice is very common in newborns, and most of it is harmless. A baby's liver is still learning to clear bilirubin, so a yellow tint often shows up around day two to four and fades over a week or two.

Here is what separates normal from worrying.

Call your pediatrician the same day if:
- Yellowing appears in the first 24 hours of life
- Your baby is very sleepy and hard to wake for feeds
- Your baby feeds poorly or has fewer wet and dirty diapers than expected
- The yellow is spreading toward the belly, arms, or legs
- Your baby's cry is high-pitched or they seem floppy or unusually fussy

How to check at home:
- Use natural daylight. Artificial bulbs can fool you. With my second son I thought he looked a little sunny under our yellow kitchen light at 6 a.m. Everyone looks sunny under that bulb.
- Gently press a finger on the nose or chest, then lift it. If the skin looks yellow instead of returning to normal color, note it.
- Look at the whites of the eyes and the gums, especially with darker skin tones, where skin color can hide yellowing.

Treatment is usually simple. Frequent feeding helps clear bilirubin, and if levels are high, phototherapy (special blue light) works very well.

The most important thing I can tell you: if your gut says something is off, call. Nobody will fault you for it, and your eyes will miss it sometimes.
