## QUORA
**Answer this question:** What newborn products are actually worth buying on Black Friday?

I'm a NICU nurse and a mother of three, so I've seen newborn gear from both the clinical side and the 2 a.m. exhausted-parent side. My honest answer: buy what gets you sleep and calm, not what has the slickest ad.

I learned this the expensive way. With my second baby I paid around $289 for a sock monitor at 2 a.m. The sock kept slipping half off a very wiggly foot, the monitor flashed red, and I spent three weeks jolting awake over false alarms. The device wasn't a scam. It just didn't match how real babies move, and it made me more anxious instead of less.

Here is how I'd decide what's worth the money on a sale weekend.

First, put safe sleep at the top of the list. A proper firm sleep surface and a simple, safe setup do more for your baby and for your own rest than any gadget.

Second, be skeptical of anything that sends alerts. If it triggers false alarms, you'll stop trusting it, and then it's wasted money on top of the stress.

Third, choose products you'll use every single day. Feeding, diapering, and soothing items earn their cost because they're used constantly. A discount on something that sits in a closet is not a deal.

Fourth, check the return policy before you buy. Newborns surprise you, and what works for one baby may not work for the next.

Finally, remember that you do not need everything. Most families need far less than the registry checklists suggest.
