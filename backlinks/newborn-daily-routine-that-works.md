I'm Melissa Torres, a NICU nurse and mother of three, and I can tell you the best routine is one you'll actually follow—which usually means it's not as rigid as you think it needs to be.

Most new parents assume newborn routines need to be military-grade. By day nine postpartum with my second daughter, I had a color-coded spreadsheet printed and taped to my kitchen cabinet. What actually works is a loose, repeatable rhythm. Your baby doesn't need exact times; they need patterns they can recognize. Feed → diaper → awake time → sleep. When babies start anticipating what comes next, they settle. When you stop fighting against variability and build flexibility into your framework, you feel like a functioning human again.

The goal is structure that accommodates life, not structure that fights against it. Some days feeding happens at 8am, some days 8:25am. That's okay. Your baby learns the rhythm, not the exact clock time.
