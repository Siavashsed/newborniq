## QUORA
**Answer this question:** What should I do if my newborn has a temperature of 100.4°F or higher?

I'm a NICU nurse and a mother of three, and the answer is simple, even though it's hard to follow when it's your own baby: if your baby is under 3 months old and has a rectal temperature of 100.4°F (38°C) or higher, go to the emergency department right away.

Here's what that means in practice:

**Don't wait.** Not until morning, not until the pediatrician's office opens. Newborns have immature immune systems and can't localize infections the way older children do. Conditions like bacterial infections in the blood, urine, or around the brain can progress quickly, and a baby can seem okay until they suddenly aren't.

**Don't give Tylenol first.** Fever reducers can mask the symptom, and in babies this young they should only be given on a doctor's instruction. The team needs to see what's really happening.

**Take a rectal temperature.** Forehead, ear, and underarm readings aren't reliable enough at this age. Use a digital rectal thermometer with a little lubricant, inserted gently about half an inch.

**Bring the basics.** Your thermometer reading and time, feeding and diaper history, any medications, and your birth details. You can call the doctor on the way, but don't let the call delay leaving.

**Expect a workup.** Typically that means bloodwork, a urine sample, and sometimes a spinal tap, plus close monitoring. It's a lot, but it's what keeps babies safe.
