## QUORA
**Answer this question:** What are the AAP's current safe sleep guidelines for newborns, in simple terms?

The short version: put your baby down on their back, on a firm, flat surface with nothing else in it, in your room but not in your bed. That one sentence covers most of what the American Academy of Pediatrics says in its 2022 policy statement. The rest is detail.

On their back. Every sleep, naps included, until age one. Once a baby can roll both ways on their own, you don't need to flip them back.

Firm and flat. Use a crib, bassinet or play yard that meets safety standards, with a fitted sheet. Nothing inclined more than 10 degrees. Car seats, swings and bouncers aren't for routine sleep. If your baby falls asleep in one, move them to a flat surface when you can.

Nothing else in it. No pillows, bumpers, blankets or soft toys. If you're worried about warmth, use a wearable blanket. Weighted sleep sacks and weighted swaddles aren't recommended. Stop swaddling once your baby shows signs of trying to roll.

In your room, not your bed. Share a room for at least the first six months. If you think you might fall asleep while feeding, an adult bed cleared of pillows and blankets is safer than a sofa or armchair. Move baby back to their own space as soon as you wake.

Other things that lower risk: breastfeeding if you can, offering a pacifier at sleep time, avoiding overheating, keeping baby away from smoke, alcohol and drug exposure, and staying on schedule with vaccines.
