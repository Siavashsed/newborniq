## QUORA
**Answer this question:** Why does my newborn keep slipping off my breast while nursing?

This is one of the most common issues I see, and the good news is it's usually fixable once you understand what's causing it.

When a baby keeps slipping off, it's typically a positioning or latch issue rather than a supply problem. The most common culprits are: the baby's mouth isn't opened wide enough before latching, the baby is tilted away from your body instead of chest-to-chest, or the baby's lower lip isn't flanged outward properly to create a seal.

Here's what actually works: Before each feeding, make sure your baby's body is aligned with yours—chest to chest, not just face to breast. Support the breast with your hand in a "C-hold" and wait for your baby to open their mouth wide (like a yawn) before bringing them to the breast. The entire areola should be in their mouth, not just the nipple. You should feel gentle tugging, not pinching or pain.

Position matters enormously too. Try the football hold if you're struggling—it gives you better visibility and control. Some babies also latch better when reclined or semi-reclined positions are used.

If you're experiencing pain or your baby seems frustrated, don't hesitate to work with a lactation consultant in person. They can watch your actual technique and make real-time adjustments.
