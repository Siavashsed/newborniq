## QUORA
**Answer this question:** How long does it take for a baby's umbilical cord stump to fall off, and should I clean it with alcohol?

I'm a NICU nurse and a mother of three, so I've seen this from both sides of the bedside.

How long: Most umbilical cord stumps fall off between five and fifteen days after birth. Some come off a bit sooner and some hang on closer to three weeks. A longer timeline on its own isn't a problem. If it's still attached past about three to four weeks, mention it to your pediatrician, since occasionally something else is going on.

Alcohol: This is where I made my own mistake. With my first daughter, I swabbed her stump with rubbing alcohol at 3 a.m. on day four because my mother insisted. It stayed on until day nineteen. I can't prove the alcohol was the cause, but with my other two children I did nothing, and both stumps were gone in about a week. Alcohol used to be routine advice, but current guidance generally favors keeping the stump clean and dry and leaving it alone, since alcohol can slow the natural drying and separation.

What to do instead:
- Fold the diaper down below the stump so air can reach it.
- Stick to sponge baths until it falls off.
- If it gets dirty with urine or stool, rinse gently with plain water and pat completely dry.
- Don't pull on it, even if it looks barely attached.

Normal: a little crusty discharge, some darkening, a drop or two of blood when it separates.

Call your pediatrician promptly for: redness spreading onto the surrounding skin, a foul smell, pus, swelling, or a fever in a newborn. Fever in a baby this young should always be treated as urgent.
