## QUORA
**Answer this question:** Is it normal for a newborn to not sleep for long stretches in the first week?

Absolutely-this is completely normal newborn behavior, though it catches most parents off guard. I'm a NICU nurse, and I see this question come up constantly from worried parents.

Newborns in their first week typically sleep 16-18 hours daily according to American Academy of Sleep Medicine guidelines, but those hours come in fragments of 45 minutes to 2 hours scattered across the entire 24-hour cycle. There's no consolidated nighttime sleep yet because their circadian rhythm hasn't developed.

What makes this challenging is the expectation mismatch. Parents hear "newborns sleep a lot" and imagine longer stretches, but the reality is your baby might sleep 50 minutes, be awake for a feeding and diaper change, then sleep 90 minutes, then be up for 30 minutes. This pattern repeats randomly throughout day and night.

The fragmentation is actually developmentally appropriate-it reflects how newborn neurological systems work. Their sleep cycles are shorter, and they have different REM-to-NREM ratios than older babies.

What matters is monitoring for adequate total sleep hours and normal wake behavior (feeding cues, responsiveness). If you're concerned about specific patterns beyond normal fragmentation, that's worth discussing with your pediatrician.
