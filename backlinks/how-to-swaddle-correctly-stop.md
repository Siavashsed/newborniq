## QUORA
**Answer this question:** What is the correct way to swaddle a newborn, and how tight should a swaddle be?

As a NICU nurse with twelve years of experience, I've wrapped thousands of newborns, and I can tell you that proper swaddling technique is both simpler and more nuanced than most parents realize.

The correct approach starts with a square blanket. Fold the top corner down, place your baby's head just above that fold, then bring one side across their body and tuck it snugly under their back. Repeat with the other side. The bottom fold comes up and over, securing everything. The key is the tension: snug enough that they can't wiggle free, but loose enough that you can fit two fingers between the blanket and their chest.

At the hips, this is critical: you need room for their legs to bend naturally. This is where I see the most mistakes. Parents wrap too tightly, thinking it creates better calming, but it actually restricts the hip flexibility babies need for healthy development and increases dysplasia risk.

Your baby should be able to move their hips and legs freely. If you can only fit one finger in, or none at all, it's too tight.

The other piece most people miss is knowing when to stop swaddling entirely.
